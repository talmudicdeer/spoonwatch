## Spoonwatch

### Winners of the Wooden Spoon: Ottawa Redblacks (0-11)

They're not *mathematically* guaranteed to win, but I'm pretty comfortable calling time of death. Hamilton is the only other team that can feasibly overtake them, and as poor as the Ticats' offense has been (more on them later), they would need to lose to this defense twice. Probably not happening. It's not like things have been sunshine and rainbows for this side in recent memory--it was just last year that I described them as 'if the wooden spoon was a franchise'--but this season has to have fans wondering if the Redblacks have been on the receiving end of a bad week from a particularly wrathful god.

I really don't know who on God's diminishingly green Earth thought Jake Maier was a net positive move from Dru Brown and Dustin Crum. He's barely a net lateral move at *QB2*, let alone the starting job. This team has pieces, but the completely wrong ones in the places they absolutely can't afford to have the wrong pieces in. As with every season this decade, we find ourselves asking if the people in charge of this team actually know how to run a sports franchise.

A big reason I ended up putting off Spoonwatch for weeks, besides life, was... how do I write about this team? Yeah, they're bad. They're extremely bad. What can I say that isn't just saying what everyone already knows at this point? With the exception of one game, they either lose by less than a score, or by no less than 20. They either tantalize with potential, only to bottle horrifically in the dying moments, or just straight up don't play that day.

The question I think a lot of people are asking is if this team could be the one to grant the 2003 Ticats a sense of release unto death. Pythagoras says yes; their current win expectation per Pythagoras is 1.63, which is a whole 0.34 games less than what the '03 Ticats finished with. The reason for this is simple: Ottawa's offense is averaging, presently, somewhere around a touchdown more per game than the '03 Ticats (22.45 for Ottawa vs 16.28 for Hamilton), but Ottawa's defense is averaging some of the most eye-watering numbers we've seen in the league in a very, very long time. Not even the '03 Ticats or '94 Pirates comes close; for that we have to look to the '95 Rough Riders, who went 3-15 (but who, curiously, Pythagoras also gave a final win expectation lower than the '03 Ticats, at 1.85 wins). If these trends hold or worsen, the 2026 Redblacks would finish with the fewest Pythagorean wins since the mid-90s, eclipsing the '94 Pirates (the current record-holders) by a whole 0.17 wins, which in the scale this metric deals in is a wide margin.

### Everyone Else, from Bad to Good

**Hamilton Tiger-Cats (4-8):** I don't know if I've seen hard evidence of how important one person was to a team quite like the chart of Hamilton's offensive production after Bo Levi Mitchell got hurt. At their peak, the Ticats were tied for 3rd in PS/G and averaging 35 PS/G. Since then, Hamilton's PS/G has dropped by a net **11.67 PS/G.** That is **insane.** They have only posted one continuous increase since, from 23.38 to 23.7 PS/G between Weeks 9 and 11. It has since dropped two weeks in a row to 23.33 PS/G, their lowest point yet. They have been ranked 8th or 9th in PS/G since Week 7, with only the aforementioned Redblacks keeping them from the basement. The remainder of the season has some good (Ottawa twice), some not good (Edmonton twice), and some in the middle (Toronto and Montreal once each). There's enough here to pull the Ticats from bad to middling, but it's still going to be an extremely disappointing season after it started with so much hope and promise, and probably not enough to qualify Hamilton for the playoffs.

**Toronto Argonauts (5-6):** Back in the halcyon days of May, when Toronto was... I wouldn't say consensus, but definitely a favorite pick for the Spoon, I remember one commenter saying that Chad Kelly was the sole reason this team wouldn't go 0-18. Looking at how this season has gone for Toronto, I can't say that was *entirely* inaccurate. Toronto's defense has been abysmal, consistently ranked near the bottom for the majority of season. Their offense hasn't been spectacular either, but it's been enough to keep the team afloat, and that really is thanks to Kelly putting this offense on his back. He's ranked second only to Davis Alexander in most passing metrics. I will say, I am quite surprised. I am not used to players missing a considerable amount of time and coming back to do quite well. Remaining schedule for the Argos is Ottawa twice, and Hamilton, BC, Edmonton, Montreal, and Winnipeg once. Probably enough to make the East semis, maybe enough to beat whoever crosses over, but would probably go out in the East Final to Montreal.

**Calgary Stampeders (5-6):** And now we enter a gaggle of teams I like to call the Who *Are* You?! Bunch: Calgary, Winnipeg, and British Columbia, in that order. Calgary, despite being one game under .500, has been ranked first in PS/G since **Week 5.** Pythagoras has named them the most underperfoming team all four weeks that I've kept the stat, giving them 2 extra wins, which is considerable. With Vernon Adams out with a head injury, though, we'll have to see what happens to that offense. Stamps fans better hope not too much, because the offense outpacing what has consistently been the 7th or 8th-ranked defense in the league for the majority of the season has been what's kept this team trending in a positive direction, and their games without Adams did not inspire confidence. Remaining schedule is Edmonton twice, Ottawa twice, Saskatchewan, and Winnipeg. Really uncertain future without VA at the helm.

**Winnipeg Blue Bombers (6-5):** You have to go back a long, long time before you can come across a season, or a batch of seasons at that, where you could confidently say that the best player in the CFL was a running back. Every once in a while, I'm tempted to make that pronouncement about Brady Oliveira. As uncertain as the future looks for Winnipeg's quarterbacks (grabbing Dru Brown was extremely Promethean in hindsight), the league's best back (in yardage, at least) has kept things rumbling. Unfortunately, a running game requires a defense to *really* lock in, and while the Bombers have been solidly middle of the road on defense all season, it has not been good enough to make up for a ground-and-pound approach, but only slightly so. Winnipeg's ranking of last in red zone touchdowns is not helping matters. Remaining schedule is Saskatchewan twice, Alberta and BC once, and Toronto and Montreal once.

**British Columbia Lions (6-5):** I've yet to talk about my Most Improved Side when measuring based on past seasons, but if there is a Most Improved award measured from how the team started to where they are now, far and away it would be the Lions. The engine behind this transformation is not the offense, as one might suspect, but their weakest component last year: the defense. From a season high of almost 38 PA/G in Week 5, the Lions have cut that down by over 11 PA/G since, now ranking 2nd in the league going into Labour Day Weekend. The last two weeks have also seen the Lions' long march from the basement in their margin of victory, posting positive point differentials for the first time all year. Montreal twice is a tough ask but then playing every other team in the league besides Edmonton and Hamilton could be a leg up, especially if the offense consistently improves like it has been for the past few weeks.

**Saskatchewan Roughriders (7-4):** What is there to say, really, about a team that has consistently ranked in the top 4 in all three tracked statistics since the season reached full swing, has performed comfortably within their Pythagorean margin, and doesn't particularly stand out in any way besides being comfortably good? Corey Mace is coaching very well this year, with only a few hiccups along the way, and the performances from their quarterbacks in regular and short-yardage duties are at or near the top. Winnipeg and Montreal twice and then Calgary, Edmonton, and BC once is a hard end to the year, but if the Riders can finish strong they'll be in good position for the West playoffs.

**Edmonton Elks (8-3):** Where the hell did *you* come from? I have many, many feelings about this team, given that my first season as an Elks fan was 2021, but that's really all I can ask. The offense has been thoroughly mid in terms of scoring, although the banes of the league are in the details. The defense, however, has been ranked first in the league every week except for two. This team is exceptionally good. Like, could make the Grey Cup good. I refuse to make any sort of statement beyond that. I'm not going to jinx it. The Elks' remaining schedule is exceedingly kind as well, as far as we've seen: Stamps twice, Ticats twice, Argos, Riders, Bombers. The Elks have only skipped the West Semi three times in the 21st century, and this has the very high potential to be the fourth.

**Montreal Alouettes (9-2):** Well, getting blown out by Winnipeg isn't the best reputation to have coming into this article, but it's not like that's how the Als have played to this point. The Als have won a lot this year, and have won *big*. Montreal have won their games by an average of over a score for the last month and change, and while that could take a dip in their runabout with BC, their remaining schedule is favorable to that trend continuing. Davis Alexander is a very, very firm MOP frontrunner at this point in time and given how weak the East looks this year, it's hard not to see this team in the Grey Cup.
